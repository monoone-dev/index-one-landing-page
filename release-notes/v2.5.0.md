*Originally released on 2026-09-04.*

Two things in this release: a Related picker that shows you where things actually live, and a retry
that no longer forgets who was speaking.

## Related links now open on a hierarchy, not a flat list

Linking a note, a recording or a document to something else used to mean scrolling one long list of
titles with no sense of where anything sat. It is now the real hierarchy — Not classified, your
Spaces and folders, and any Shared Ivys — and it opens **centred on the item you are already
looking at**, so you start from where you are rather than at the top of everything.

- **Search covers the path, not just the title.** Typing `Product / Roadmap` finds the folder, not
  only items whose own name contains those words.
- **Big groups load as you reach them**, so a Space with hundreds of items opens immediately.
- **Keyboard all the way through** — the list, the groups and the confirmation.
- **A link to a Space or folder links to that container and nothing else.** It never quietly fans out
  to everything inside it, and it stays exactly one relation.
- **Locked content stays locked.** Titles, counts, paths and previews behind a lock are as invisible
  here as everywhere else, including in search totals.

Container links now also survive the trash: move something to the trash and restore it, and its
relations come back with it. A Shared document that was permanently deleted stays deleted — restoring
cannot resurrect it.

## Retrying a failed recording keeps Me and Others apart

A recording that failed to transcribe could always be retried, but the recovered transcript came back
as one unattributed voice. The reason was the archive itself: it mixes your microphone and the system
capture into a single track, so the speaker split is gone before a retry can look at it.

Failed recordings now keep each side as its own track, and a retry transcribes them separately and
merges them exactly as a live recording does — so `Me` and `Others` survive. The extra audio is kept
only for recordings that actually failed, is released as soon as a retry succeeds, and is sealed with
your folder like everything else. Older recordings, or ones whose extra tracks the storage limit has
already reclaimed, still retry the way they did before.
